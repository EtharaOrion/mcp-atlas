# The 1900 district return, for Friday's committee

Marion here. Committee papers close on Friday, and the number at the top of this
one gets read out in the room and then quoted back at us for years.

We are stating how much money Congress actually
appropriated for work at light-stations in one fiscal year, exactly as that is
printed in the Light-House Board's own annual report for the year. Peverell went
through the scans last week and keyed a working schedule off them, twenty four
lines, and every line names the page it came from. He was quick rather than
careful. He dropped nearly everything into the first heading because that was
the column already open on his screen, and he never went back over his typing.

The pages came with this brief, filed by page number. Eight of them. Six carry
entries of the kind we are after and two do not, and working out which two is
part of the job. There is a scan of the volume's front matter in there as well
so the committee can see which book the pages come from. Those images are the
evidence. They are photographs of a bound volume taken at a reading room table,
and nothing on them has been cleaned up, straightened or retyped. A figure that
is not legible on a scan is not available to you at all, and the volume will not
be fetched out again for us.

Do this in order, because each part feeds the next.

Read the standard first. It sits in the notebook with the other standing papers,
and you want the third edition, which came into force at the start of August.
There is a second edition next to it. It says something quite different about
which acts count and about how floating plant is treated, and it is superseded.
Work from the current one, and read it before you touch a line rather than after.

Then take the schedule line by line against the page each line names. This is the
part that is easy to get wrong, and the reason I am not simply asking Peverell
to total his own column. The report states money in four different ways
inside a single paragraph and the only thing separating them is the verb. Money
that a named act actually appropriated is one thing. Money that somebody
estimates the work will cost is another. Money the Board is asking for is a
third. A ceiling, a most that it may cost, is a fourth. All four look identical
once they have been keyed into a column headed amount, and so, I am sorry to
say, do gallons, running feet and horsepower. Several of those are sitting in
the schedule as though they were dollars. File every line under the heading the
standard gives it. Where the page and the line disagree about a figure, the page
is right and the line gets put right. Leave alone whatever Peverell already got
right, and there is more of that than you would expect.

Watch the dates on the acts. The report talks freely about money granted years
ago and still being spent, and that money belongs to an earlier return rather
than to this one. Watch also for the same amount turning up at four different
stations across two pages. Those are four separate grants made by one act on one
day, not one grant mentioned four times, and collapsing them costs us most of a
district.

Once the schedule is right the total falls out of it. On top of the total I need
a bridge. Start from every money figure printed on those pages, take out each
heading in turn, and land on the figure we are returning. The bridge is what the
committee actually argues about, so build it out of the pages.

Give it to me as one page that opens in a browser with nothing running behind it.
Not a note, not a message, not something I have to convert first. It wants the
headline figure where the eye lands first, the bridge drawn as a chart, a
second chart splitting the total by district, and a table of every line showing
its page, the wording that decided it, the heading it ended under and whether it
counts. I must be able to sort and filter that table.

Then tell me the total in plain words.

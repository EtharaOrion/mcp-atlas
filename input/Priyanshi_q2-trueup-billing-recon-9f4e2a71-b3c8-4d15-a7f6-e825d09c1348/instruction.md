The true-up math itself is pretty simple: figure out how much each account's actual usage exceeded their committed quarterly minimum, multiply that overage by their contracted rate, then subtract any applicable credits. If an account came in under their minimum, they owe nothing.

For actual usage numbers, pull from the billing platform. One thing to watch: amounts there are stored as integer cents, so divide by 100 before doing any math. Only count charges with a succeeded status and a created date that falls inside the April through June window. For contract terms, committed minimums, rates, and overage caps, the CRM is the authoritative source. If anything else gives you a different number, the CRM wins. Signed amendments might also show up in the data folder, so cross-check those against the CRM when you find them. The accounting system in your environment is just a mirror of the billing platform for reconciliation purposes and is not an independent usage source.

SLA credits are only valid if there is a signed VP authorization behind them. There is an authorization document in the data folder, and that is where the approved credit amount lives. You can use the incident system to look up the incident details, but for the actual credit figure, go to the signed document.

One thing that catches people: effective dates on contract modifications. Something can be fully signed before Q2 closes and still not apply to Q2 usage. Check those dates carefully.
